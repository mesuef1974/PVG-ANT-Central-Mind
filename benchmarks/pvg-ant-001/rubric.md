# PVG–ANT Benchmark 001 — Rubric

**Benchmark type:** governed kernel-coverage and diagnostic baseline.  
**Important:** this is not an empirical score for a language model and not evidence that the mind can answer unseen questions without evaluation. It measures how completely the current translation registry already contains the gold route and the required warnings.

## Dimensions

Each case is scored from 0 to 2 on six dimensions:

1. **Exact forward translation**
   - 2: exact route is already represented.
   - 1: route requires composition or an unstated refinement.
   - 0: missing family.
2. **Controlled reverse translation**
   - 2: inverse and its noninjectivity are explicit.
   - 1: reverse conclusion is only partially encoded.
   - 0: reverse discipline is absent.
3. **Preserved/lost information**
   - 2: loss class and consequence are explicit.
   - 1: loss is mentioned but not operational.
   - 0: information loss is ignored.
4. **ANT tool routing**
   - 2: correct tool and trigger are available.
   - 1: only a neighboring tool is available.
   - 0: the required method family is missing.
5. **Wall and missing certificate**
   - 2: precise wall and certificate are available.
   - 1: generic analytic warning only.
   - 0: no certificate discipline.
6. **Honest classification**
   - 2: known/identity/diagnostic/candidate/theorem boundary is correct.
   - 1: classification needs priority review.
   - 0: overclaim risk is unguarded.

Maximum per case: **12**.

## Domains

The benchmark contains exactly ten cases in each domain:

- geometry/arithmetic;
- local analytic Bell/Euler structure;
- Mellin/Perron and transfer;
- residue/character Fourier analysis;
- sieve information and parity;
- probabilistic additive functions and exceptional sets.

## Case classes

The suite includes direct and reverse translation, mixed-card composition, deceptive analogy, phase/label loss, smooth/sharp normalization, uniformity traps, classification traps, and cases where PVG is decorative or insufficient.

## Interpretation

- A high coverage score means the registry contains the right conceptual route.
- It does not prove autonomous reasoning performance.
- A low score identifies a missing card, missing composition rule, or missing certificate interface.
- Pass 002 must be selected from benchmark failures rather than from a desire to increase card count.
