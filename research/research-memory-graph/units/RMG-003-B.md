## 1. Mission

Extend the character and L-function layer to prime-distribution observables in reduced residue classes while separating exact finite Fourier reconstruction from asymptotic prime-distribution theorems.

## 2. Core observables

For `gcd(a,q)=1`, define

\[
\psi(x;q,a)=\sum_{\substack{n\le x\\n\equiv a\pmod q}}\Lambda(n).
\]

For a Dirichlet character `chi mod q`, define

\[
\psi(x,\chi)=\sum_{n\le x}\chi(n)\Lambda(n).
\]

Character orthogonality gives the exact finite identity

\[
\psi(x;q,a)=\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\overline{\chi(a)}\,\psi(x,\chi).
\]

This is a reconstruction identity, not a proof of equidistribution.

## 3. PVG translation

The observable `psi(x;q,a)` is the logarithmically weighted mass of all positive single-axis PVG points whose decoded prime power lies in residue channel `a mod q` and below the integer cutoff `x`.

The twisted observable `psi(x,chi)` is the same single-axis mass weighted by the character phase of the decoded residue.

Translation class:

```text
EXACT_IDENTITY_REWRITE for finite observables
PVG_RESTATEMENT for PNT-AP
DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY for zero-free and GRH layers
```

Preserved:

- prime-power support;
- logarithmic weights;
- residue class;
- character phase;
- finite Fourier reconstruction.

Not generated by the translation:

- asymptotic cancellation in nonprincipal channels;
- analytic continuation of L-functions;
- zero-free regions;
- exclusion or control of exceptional real zeros;
- GRH.

## 4. Principal versus nonprincipal channels

The principal character organizes the main term after analytic input. Nonprincipal channels must be controlled analytically to prove cancellation.

The distinction is essential:

```text
orthogonality identifies channels
!=
analytic estimates control channels
```

## 5. PNT in arithmetic progressions

The known theorem for fixed `q` and reduced `a` is

\[
\psi(x;q,a)\sim\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}.
\]

In this unit it is imported as a literature-certified theorem and represented in PVG as asymptotic equalization of logarithmic single-axis mass across reduced residue channels.

No new proof or improved error term is claimed.

## 6. Certificate ladder

### Level 0 — finite identity

Character orthogonality reconstructs `psi(x;q,a)` exactly from `psi(x,chi)`.

### Level 1 — classical zero-free control

A suitable zero-free region for Dirichlet L-functions supports an unconditional quantitative PNT-AP error term for fixed modulus, with the usual dependence on constants and uniformity range.

### Level 2 — exceptional-zero boundary

A possible exceptional real zero creates a nonuniform bias mechanism in real-character channels. PVG residue channels can display the resulting imbalance only after the analytic data are supplied; they do not resolve the phenomenon.

### Level 3 — GRH-conditional layer

GRH yields square-root-scale error terms up to logarithmic factors. This layer is conditional and records no project progress toward GRH.
