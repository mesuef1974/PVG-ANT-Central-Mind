# TKG-010 — Zero Symmetries, Trivial-Zero Routing, and Critical-Strip Taxonomy

## Status

`IMPLEMENTED — VERIFIER COMMITTED — EXECUTION NOT CLAIMED`

## Purpose

Build a governed reasoning layer for classifying zeros and listing the analytic certificates required before an explicit formula may be invoked. This unit does not compute, certify, or locate zeros.

## Core taxonomy

For a completed primitive Dirichlet L-function:

- absolute-convergence region: `Re(s)>1`;
- critical strip: `0<Re(s)<1`;
- critical line: `Re(s)=1/2`;
- left region: `Re(s)<=0`.

These labels organize the complex plane. They are not zero theorems.

## Symmetry routing

Under the known functional-equation hypotheses, a zero `rho` is routed to `1-conj(rho)` for the conjugate character. With real coefficients, conjugation supplies the usual quartet

```text
rho, conj(rho), 1-rho, 1-conj(rho).
```

A pair with real parts `0.3` and `0.7` is symmetric about `1/2` but lies off the critical line. Hence symmetry alone does not imply RH or GRH.

## Trivial zeros

The Gamma factor and character parity route the standard negative-integer progressions:

```text
even parity -> negative even progression
odd parity  -> negative odd progression
```

The exact character, principal exceptions, and completed-function convention must still be checked. The harness returns candidates, not analytic certificates.

## Explicit-formula prerequisite graph

Before an explicit formula is authorized, the reasoning graph requires:

1. analytic or meromorphic continuation;
2. a proved functional equation;
3. a pole and trivial-zero ledger;
4. a logarithmic-derivative identity in a valid region;
5. contour-shift justification;
6. growth bounds on boundary segments;
7. a zero-sum convergence or truncation convention;
8. a test-function or endpoint protocol.

A formal contour shift without these certificates is rejected.

## PVG boundary

PVG can retain the prime-axis coefficients associated with an L-function and attach externally supplied zero metadata to that global object. Prime-valuation vectors alone do not reconstruct the Gamma factor, analytic continuation, or complex zero set. The translation is therefore hybrid and nonlocal.
