## Purpose

This unit completes the first analytic bridge of the Translation Knowledge Graph by explaining how global boundary information about transforms can, under theorem-specific side conditions, be transferred into asymptotic information about summatory functions.

It does not prove a Tauberian theorem. It does not prove the prime number theorem.

## Core distinction

A transform identity such as

\[
F(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a(n)}{n^s}
\]

is not by itself an asymptotic theorem for

\[
A(x)=\sum_{n\le x}a(n).
\]

A Tauberian transfer requires an explicit theorem variant together with its positivity, monotonicity, boundary regularity, normalization, and provenance hypotheses.

## Wiener–Ikehara-style interface

A governed interface records:

1. the nondecreasing or positive-measure summatory object;
2. its Laplace–Stieltjes or Dirichlet transform;
3. the boundary point and main polar part;
4. the regularity of the remainder after subtracting the pole;
5. the exact theorem variant;
6. the authorized conclusion and its scope.

Pole data alone are not sufficient.

## Boundary nonvanishing

For logarithmic derivatives, a zero on the boundary introduces a singularity. Thus boundary nonvanishing is not decorative; it is a load-bearing hypothesis in the route from analytic transforms to prime-distribution asymptotics.

The harness can mark a supplied boundary zero as an obstruction, but it cannot prove absence of boundary zeros.

## One-way implication ledger

Conditionally valid governed routes include:

\[
\psi(x)\sim x \Longrightarrow \theta(x)\sim x,
\]

using control of higher prime powers, and

\[
\theta(x)\sim x \Longrightarrow \pi(x)\sim \frac{x}{\log x},
\]

using partial summation.

The reverse directions are not registered automatically. Finite numerical agreement is never promoted to an asymptotic theorem.

## PVG translation

PVG contributes the arithmetic coefficient geometry:

\[
\nu(n)=ke_p \Longrightarrow \Lambda(n)=\log p.
\]

It also supports cumulative observables such as

\[
\psi(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n).
\]

The Tauberian step, however, is global and analytic. It acts on transform boundary behavior and is not a local operation on valuation coordinates.

Translation type:

```text
hybrid
nonlocal
lossy after aggregation
```

The asymptotic output does not reconstruct individual valuation points or complex zero locations.
