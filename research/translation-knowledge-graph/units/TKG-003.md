## Main identities

For an arithmetic function `f`,

\[
D_f(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{f(n)}{n^s}.
\]

When absolute convergence permits rearrangement,

\[
D_{f*g}(s)=D_f(s)D_g(s).
\]

If `f` is multiplicative, then in its valid convergence region,

\[
D_f(s)=\prod_p\left(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{f(p^k)}{p^{ks}}\right).
\]

## PVG translation

Write

\[
n=\prod_p p^{\alpha_p},\qquad \alpha=\nu(n).
\]

Then

\[
n^{-s}=\prod_p p^{-s\alpha_p}.
\]

Thus a Dirichlet series is a weighted sum over finite-support labelled valuation vectors. For multiplicative `f`, the weight factors independently along prime axes, producing one local generating factor per axis.

The translation is exact only when:

- prime coordinates remain labelled;
- coefficients remain attached to valuation points;
- the asserted infinite rearrangements occur in a justified convergence region.

## Worked examples

### Divisor function

Since

\[
\tau=\mathbf 1*\mathbf 1,
\]

we obtain

\[
D_\tau(s)=\zeta(s)^2\qquad (\Re s>1).
\]

At one prime axis,

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\tau(p^k)x^k
=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)x^k
=(1-x)^{-2}.
\]

For `n=12=2^2 3`, the valuation point is `(2,1)`, its divisor box has `(2+1)(1+1)=6` lattice points, and `tau(12)=6`.

### Möbius function

The local axis sequence is

```text
mu(1)=1, mu(p)=-1, mu(p^k)=0 for k>=2.
```

Hence the local factor is `1-p^{-s}` and

\[
D_\mu(s)=\frac1{\zeta(s)}\qquad (\Re s>1).
\]

This formula does not authorize continuation through zeros or any zero-free-region claim.

### Multiplicative but not completely multiplicative

The function `tau` is multiplicative on coprime inputs, but

\[
\tau(4)=3\neq4=\tau(2)^2.
\]

PVG explanation: coprime multiplication adds disjoint-support valuation vectors, while `2·2` adds on the same axis. Ordinary multiplicativity only governs disjoint axes.
