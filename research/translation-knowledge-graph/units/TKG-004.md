## Purpose

Turn Euler factors into a reasoning route that recovers prime-power coefficients and explains exactly why the von Mangoldt function appears in the logarithmic derivative of zeta.

## Core identities

For a multiplicative Dirichlet series

\[
D_f(s)=\prod_p F_p(p^{-s}),
\]

its logarithmic derivative decomposes axis by axis:

\[
-\frac{D_f'(s)}{D_f(s)}=-\frac{d}{ds}\log D_f(s).
\]

For zeta,

\[
-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge1}(\log p)p^{-ks}
=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s},
\qquad \Re(s)>1.
\]

## PVG reasoning route

- a point `k e_p` lies on one labelled prime axis;
- logarithmic differentiation contributes the axis label `log p`;
- the weight is constant along the nonzero points of that axis;
- points with support cardinality greater than one receive coefficient zero.

Thus the coefficient recovery rule is exact:

```text
support(nu(n)) = 1  -> coefficient log p
support(nu(n)) > 1  -> coefficient 0
```

## Generalized mechanism

If

\[
D(s)=\zeta(s)^a,
\]

then

\[
-\frac{D'(s)}{D(s)}
=a\left(-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}\right),
\]

so the coefficient at `n` is `a Lambda(n)`.

Examples:

- `D=ζ`: coefficient `Λ(n)`;
- `D=ζ²`: coefficient `2Λ(n)`;
- `D=1/ζ`: coefficient `-Λ(n)`.
