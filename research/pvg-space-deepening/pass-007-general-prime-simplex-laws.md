# PASS-007 — General Prime-Simplex Laws

**Program:** `PVG-INVERSE-GEOMETRY-001`  
**Status:** exact elementary identities plus executable verification  
**Scope:** arbitrary finite ordered prime set `p_1<...<p_m`, `m>=2`  
**Scientific class:** identity / reinterpretation / finite verification; no originality or theorem-priority claim

---

## 1. Purpose

The previous passes treated points, edges, triangles, and tetrahedra separately. This pass installs one common grammar for a simplex generated by

\[
P=(p_1,\ldots,p_m),\qquad p_1<\cdots<p_m.
\]

The resulting support face has dimension \(m-1\), horizontal root lattice \(A_{m-1}\), and a complete graph on its prime-axis vertices.

---

## 2. Combinatorial skeleton

A \(k\)-dimensional face uses \(k+1\) vertices. Therefore

\[
f_k=\binom{m}{k+1},\qquad 0\le k\le m-1.
\]

In particular:

\[
\#\text{vertices}=m,
\]

\[
\#\text{edges}=\binom m2,
\]

and the total number of nonempty faces is

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{m}{k+1}=2^m-1.
\]

At a relative-interior lattice point of an \(m\)-axis face, the ordered primitive horizontal degree is

\[
m(m-1),
\]

corresponding to all transfers \(e_j-e_i\), \(i\ne j\).

---

## 3. Ratio transport is path-independent

Every directed edge \(p_i\to p_j\) carries ratio

\[
R_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

For any path

\[
i_0\to i_1\to\cdots\to i_t,
\]

we have

\[
\prod_{a=0}^{t-1}R_{i_ai_{a+1}}
=
\frac{p_{i_t}}{p_{i_0}}.
\]

Thus every closed path has multiplicative holonomy \(1\). The ratio system is an exact multiplicative cocycle determined entirely by endpoints.

---

## 4. Difference coordinates have rank \(m-1\)

Define consecutive gaps

\[
d_i=p_{i+1}-p_i,\qquad 1\le i\le m-1.
\]

Then every pair difference is a consecutive-gap sum:

\[
p_j-p_i=d_i+d_{i+1}+\cdots+d_{j-1}.
\]

Hence all \(\binom m2\) pair differences are generated by only \(m-1\) independent coordinates.

The gap vector is invariant under common translation:

\[
(p_1+c,\ldots,p_m+c)
\mapsto
(d_1,\ldots,d_{m-1}).
\]

It therefore records shape but not absolute position. One anchor reconstructs the vertices:

\[
p_k=p_1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}d_i.
\]

---

## 5. Labeled pair sums reconstruct all vertices for \(m\ge3\)

Let

\[
s_{ij}=p_i+p_j.
\]

Write

\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^m p_i,
\qquad
E=\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}.
\]

Each vertex occurs in exactly \(m-1\) pair sums, so

\[
E=(m-1)S,
\]

and therefore

\[
S=\frac{E}{m-1}.
\]

For a fixed vertex define its incident sum

\[
I_i=\sum_{j\ne i}s_{ij}.
\]

Then

\[
I_i=(m-2)p_i+S,
\]

hence for \(m\ge3\):

\[
\boxed{
p_i=\frac{I_i-S}{m-2}.
}
\]

Thus the complete labeled pair-sum transform is injective for every simplex dimension at least two. The edge case \(m=2\) is exceptional: one sum cannot recover two separate vertices.

---

## 6. GCD of all pair sums

For distinct prime vertices and \(m\ge3\):

\[
\boxed{
\gcd_{i<j}(p_i+p_j)=
\begin{cases}
1,&2\in P,\\
2,&2\notin P.
\end{cases}
}
\]

If all vertices are odd, every pair sum is even. No odd common divisor can remain because

\[
(p_i+p_j)+(p_i+p_k)-(p_j+p_k)=2p_i,
\]

and distinct primes cannot all share one odd divisor.

If \(2\in P\), then the sums \(2+p_j\) are odd, while sums between odd vertices are even, forcing global gcd \(1\).

---

## 7. General axis-2 routing law

Every edge produces two reduced transitions: its sum and absolute difference.

### All vertices odd

Every sum and difference is even. Therefore all

\[
2\binom m2=m(m-1)
\]

reduced transitions contain axis \(2\).

### The simplex contains axis \(2\)

There are \(m-1\) edges incident to \(2\). Their two transitions are odd, giving

\[
2(m-1)
\]

transitions excluding axis \(2\).

The remaining

\[
\binom{m-1}{2}
\]

odd-odd edges each give two even transitions, hence

\[
2\binom{m-1}{2}=(m-1)(m-2)
\]

transitions containing axis \(2\).

The counts close exactly:

\[
2(m-1)+(m-1)(m-2)=m(m-1).
\]

---

## 8. Level-preserving edge laws

For an edge \((p_i,p_j)\):

- sum preserves \(\Omega\) iff \(p_i+p_j\) is prime;
- difference preserves \(\Omega\) iff \(|p_j-p_i|\) is prime.

Because the sum of two odd primes is even and greater than \(2\), every sum-preserving edge must contain axis \(2\). Therefore all such edges are incident to one vertex, and

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{sum-preserving edges}\}\le m-1.
}
\]

This upper bound is exact as a structural ceiling; whether it is attained depends on the selected prime vertices.

For two odd prime vertices, difference preservation is equivalent to a gap of \(2\).

## 9. Normalized-gap composition

Define

\[
\delta(a,b)=\frac{b-a}{b+a}.
\]

For every ordered triple \(a<b<c\):

\[
\boxed{
\delta(a,c)
=
\frac{\delta(a,b)+\delta(b,c)}
{1+\delta(a,b)\delta(b,c)}.
}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
\theta(a,b)=\frac12\log\frac ba,
\]

we have

\[
\theta(a,c)=\theta(a,b)+\theta(b,c),
\qquad
\delta(a,b)=\tanh\theta(a,b).
\]

Thus ratio composition, log-distance addition, and normalized-gap composition are three equivalent coordinate descriptions of the same endpoint law.

| Number of axes \(m\) | Support simplex | Root lattice | Edges | Pair-sum recovery |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | point/ray generator | \(A_0\) | 0 | not applicable |
| 2 | segment | \(A_1\) | 1 | not injective from one sum |
| 3 | triangle | \(A_2\) | 3 | exact |

The earlier point, edge, triangle, and tetrahedron passes are therefore instances of one finite-support simplex law.
