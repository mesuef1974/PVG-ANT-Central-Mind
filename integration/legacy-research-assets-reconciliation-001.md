## 4. Six legacy bridges accepted into the language kernel

### Bridge A — Sieve information ↔ truncated valuation information

For a sieve level `D`, the divisibility indicators

\[
I_d(n)=\mathbf 1_{d\mid n},\qquad d\le D,
\]

carry the same pointwise information as the truncated vector

\[
\nu^{(D)}(n)=
\left(
\min\left(v_p(n),\left\lfloor\frac{\log D}{\log p}\right\rfloor\right)
\right)_{p\le D}.
\]

Classification: exact known identity / structural bridge.

### Bridge B — Aggregation ↔ information loss

The pointwise data `ν^(D)(n)` and the aggregated family data

\[
A_d=\sum_{d\mid n}a_n
\]

are not equivalent. Aggregation may discard sign, phase, ordering, and target-purity information.

Classification: diagnostic information-loss bridge.

### Bridge C — Principal-character removal

For reduced residue classes, the clean fiber error subtracts the principal-character component:

\[
\mathcal E^\circ(T;q,a)=e^{-T/2}
\left(
\psi(e^T;q,a)-\frac{\psi_q(e^T)}{\varphi(q)}
\right).
\]

Classification: exact normalization bridge.

### Bridge D — Residue-fiber variance ↔ character second moment

Character orthogonality converts variance across residue fibers into a second moment over non-principal characters.

Classification: exact Parseval/orthogonality bridge, with normalization convention recorded per task.

### Bridge E — Structural layer separation

```text
support geometry
≠ convolution geometry
≠ residue geometry
≠ spectral geometry
```

Each layer preserves and loses different information. No layer transition is free.

Classification: diagnostic routing principle.

### Bridge F — Edge-error separation

For a smoothed prime-ray quantity, elementary desmoothing can isolate an edge term, while the central square-root estimate remains the true hard certificate.

Classification: known elementary estimate / barrier-location diagnostic.
