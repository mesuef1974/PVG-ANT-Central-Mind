## 1. Mission

The PVG–ANT Central Mind is not a single-problem solver and is not a Goldbach-only research package. It is the governed foundation for a future specialist intelligence in analytic number theory and prime-valuation geometry.

Its long-term target is a cumulative, auditable, extensible research mind that can:

- represent number-theoretic objects in multiple mathematical languages;
- translate between prime-valuation geometry and analytic number theory;
- choose strategies according to the requested certificate;
- track dependencies, errors, confidence, provenance, and scientific ceilings;
- preserve cumulative knowledge without collapsing discussion, implementation, validation, and theorem status;
- later supply structured concepts, reasoning traces, graph data, benchmarks, and governance constraints for a neural or hybrid neuro-symbolic system.

## 2. Core scientific domains

The central scientific identity is:

```text
Analytic Number Theory
+
Prime-Valuation Geometry
+
Governed bidirectional translation
```

Supporting domains include:

- elementary and multiplicative number theory;
- additive and probabilistic number theory;
- prime distribution;
- sieve methods;
- Dirichlet characters and L-functions;
- circle-method and Fourier interfaces;
- spectral and operator viewpoints;
- computational and experimental number theory;
- formal proof and Lean verification;
- certificate and research-release governance.

Goldbach is one application and stress test. It is not the identity of the system.

## 3. Research Mind OS architecture

```text
Layer 0 — mathematical foundations
Layer 1 — knowledge and research memory graph
Layer 2 — problem understanding and representation
Layer 3 — strategy and reasoning
Layer 4 — meta-reasoning and self-audit
Layer 5 — certificate, confidence, and governance
Layer 6 — error memory and cumulative learning
Layer 7 — research planning and capability growth
Layer 8 — future neural / neuro-symbolic interface
```

No layer may silently claim maturity merely because its specification exists.

## 4. Future neural-system readiness

The present repository is intended to become a high-quality precursor for a future neural or hybrid system. Therefore every mature component should eventually expose:

- canonical concepts and identifiers;
- typed relations and dependencies;
- positive and negative examples;
- reasoning traces;
- refusal and scientific-ceiling examples;
- certificate requirements;
- confidence labels tied to evidence;
- correction histories;
- benchmark cases;
- provenance and versioning.

The current artifacts are not yet a training corpus. Corpus use requires separate authorization, privacy review, deduplication, leakage audit, frozen schemas, and benchmark separation.

## 5. Cumulative preservation rule

Every substantive architectural decision, mathematical unit, correction, benchmark, or capability change must be translated into Git as:

```text
artifact + status + dependencies + scientific ceiling + commit
```

Conversation text alone is not repository state.

Components must not be discarded merely because a later architecture supersedes them. They must be preserved, explicitly deprecated with reasons, or linked to a successor.

## 6. Capability maturity ladder

```text
CONCEPT_DISCUSSION
```

Promotion requires evidence. Architectural completeness is not operational competence.

## 7. Scientific honesty constraints

The mind must separate:

```text
known theorem
identity
reinterpretation
diagnostic
candidate mechanism
conditional result
boundary
open problem
new theorem
```

It must not infer universal theorems from finite computation, geometric reformulation, benchmark success, or neural confidence.

## 8. Success criterion

The project succeeds when the repository becomes a reusable, interconnected, validated specialist substrate that can support:

- rigorous human research assistance;
- transparent strategy selection;
- cross-language PVG ↔ ANT translation;
- governed experimentation;
- formal and computational verification;
- future neural or neuro-symbolic training and inference.
