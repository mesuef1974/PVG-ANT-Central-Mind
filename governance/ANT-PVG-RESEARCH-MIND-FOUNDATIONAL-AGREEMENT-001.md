# ANT–PVG Research Mind Foundational Agreement 001

Status: governing agreement
Classification: Architecture / Governance
Branch: `agent/pvg-axis-sum-continuation-002`

## 1. Identity

The project shall build a specialized research mind for:

- analytic number theory (ANT);
- Prime-Valuation Geometry (PVG);
- a rigorous bidirectional translation language between ANT and PVG;
- future neural-symbolic use without training a neural network from scratch at the present stage.

The mind is not a Goldbach-only system. Goldbach, the Riemann Hypothesis, prime distribution, L-functions, sieve theory, circle method, explicit formulae, zero-density methods, prime gaps, and related themes are application families inside the broader mind.

## 2. Central mission

The mind must assimilate current ANT knowledge and learn to view it through PVG without replacing known mathematics by visual analogy.

For each accepted concept, theorem, lemma, conjecture, method, or obstruction, the mind must preserve:

1. the canonical ANT formulation;
2. equivalent formulations and hypotheses;
3. the PVG translation, when meaningful;
4. the inverse or return translation;
5. preserved information;
6. lost information and kernel;
7. numerical examples;
8. computational verification;
9. dependency and certificate structure;
10. scientific classification and ceiling.

## 3. Required cognitive functions

The architecture must progressively implement functional analogues of:

- long-term memory;
- working memory;
- attention and relevance selection;
- logical reasoning;
- problem solving;
- strategy selection;
- decision making;
- self-review;
- error memory;
- confidence calibration;
- hypothesis generation;
- scientific governance;
- learning from verified corrections.

These functions are to be explicit, inspectable, and governed. They are not claims of biological equivalence to the human brain.

## 4. Assimilation rule

A concept is not considered assimilated merely because it is mentioned or summarized.

Minimum governed assimilation path:

```text
canonical ANT definition
→ equivalent forms
→ examples and counterexamples
→ PVG representation
→ inverse-translation analysis
→ numerical verification
→ computational regression checks
→ theorem/lemma/conjecture links
→ certificate and wall analysis
→ reasoning benchmark
```

The project must retain the maturity level of every concept and must not use the word `assimilated` without qualification before numerical verification.

## 5. Four mandatory representations

Every mature knowledge object should support, where mathematically meaningful:

1. formal mathematical representation;
2. PVG geometric representation;
3. computational representation;
4. cognitive graph representation.

Visualization is valuable only when paired with a precise translation contract. A picture or geometric analogy is not by itself a theorem or equivalence.

## 6. ANT ↔ PVG translation contract

Every translation must state:

```text
source object
source assumptions
target PVG object
translation map
preserved invariants
lost information
inverse status
kernel or ambiguity
worked numerical example
computational check
scientific classification
```

Allowed inverse states include:

```text
exactly invertible
invertible on a restricted class
recoverable modulo kernel
one-way diagnostic only
not presently known
```

## 7. Prime numbers and Riemann program

Prime numbers and the Riemann Hypothesis are mandatory major families in the graph.

The prime-number family must include at least:

- prime counting and density;
- Chebyshev functions;
- von Mangoldt function;
- arithmetic progressions;
- short intervals;
- prime gaps;
- sieve methods;
- additive prime problems;
- prime powers;
- local and global PVG geometry.

The Riemann family must include at least:

- zeta function;
- Euler product;
- analytic continuation;
- functional equation;
- zero distribution;
- explicit formulae;
- prime number theorem;
- zero-free regions;
- zero-density estimates;
- moments and large values;
- pair correlation and spectral diagnostics;
- computational verification;
- PVG translation status.

No RH, GRH, Goldbach, or other open-problem progress may be claimed from architecture, visualization, finite computation, or translation alone.

## 8. Numerical and computational discipline

Definitions and translations must be accompanied by small exact examples before large experiments.

Computational checks must distinguish:

- exact identities;
- finite enumerations;
- numerical approximations;
- statistical diagnostics;
- asymptotic evidence;
- formal proofs.

A passing script is evidence for the tested claim only. It is not an all-range theorem.

## 9. Knowledge organization

The mind shall be organized primarily around mathematical objects and relations, not around books.

Books, papers, and notes feed shared objects such as:

- arithmetic functions;
- Dirichlet convolution;
- Dirichlet series;
- Euler products;
- characters and L-functions;
- exponential sums;
- sieve weights;
- explicit formulae;
- valuation fibers;
- PVG observables;
- certificates and walls.

Repeated sources must enrich the same object rather than create disconnected summaries.

## 10. Future neural-symbolic direction

The present repository is a governed symbolic and cognitive architecture. It may later support:

- retrieval systems;
- tool-using language models;
- structured supervision;
- benchmark generation;
- graph embeddings;
- fine-tuning or adapters;
- neural-symbolic planning.

However:

```text
repository content ≠ approved training corpus
research graph ≠ neural model
interface card ≠ validated learned skill
```

Any future training corpus requires separate authorization, provenance checks, contamination controls, benchmark separation, and role separation.

## 11. Cumulative preservation rule

All mature decisions, definitions, translations, examples, errors, corrections, and dependencies must be committed to Git step by step.

Conversation text alone is not considered durable project state.

A completed step requires:

```text
repository artifact
+ truthful status
+ dependency links
+ commit
+ push
```

Historical errors must be corrected transparently rather than erased from the record.

## 12. Success condition

The long-term goal is a research mind that can:

- understand a new ANT problem;
- identify the canonical objects and known results;
- select an appropriate ANT, PVG, computational, formal, or hybrid strategy;
- translate the problem into PVG when justified;
- return from PVG to ANT while reporting information loss;
- generate and verify numerical examples;
- identify the required certificate;
- detect unsupported conclusions;
- explain its reasoning and uncertainty;
- integrate verified corrections into durable memory.
