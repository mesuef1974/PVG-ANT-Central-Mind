## 1. Purpose

The exact passport answers where an integer point lives. The local-neighborhood layer asks:

1. Which points are its nearest horizontal neighbors?
2. Which axis ratios generate those neighbors?
3. Which geometric sequences pass through the point?
4. Can an observed neighbor reveal active prime axes?
5. How do addition, subtraction, gcd, and lcm transform a local edge?
6. What survives in faces with three, four, or more prime axes?

The point is treated as a node in a finite horizontal simplex, an infinite family of axis-parallel rays, one primitive composite ray, and several arithmetic transition graphs.

---

## 2. Exact setup

Let

\[
N=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{a_i},\qquad a_i>0,
\]

with support

\[
P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_s\}
\]

and level

\[
r=\Omega(N)=a_1+\cdots+a_s.
\]

The horizontal section of the face generated by \(P\) is

\[
\Sigma_r(P)=\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{x_i}:x_i\in\mathbb N_0,\ \sum_i x_i=r\right\}.
\]

Its exponent vectors are the lattice points of an \((s-1)\)-simplex.

---

## 3. Axis-ratio matrix

For every ordered pair of distinct active axes define

\[
R_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

The ratios satisfy

\[
R_{ij}R_{jk}=R_{ik},\qquad R_{ij}R_{ji}=1,
\]

and every closed axis cycle has product \(1\).

Thus axis ratios are the multiplicative image of coordinate-transfer directions.

---

## 4. Primitive horizontal neighbors

If \(a_i>0\), transfer one valuation unit from \(p_i\) to \(p_j\):

\[
N_{i\to j}=N\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

In exponent coordinates:

\[
(a_1,\ldots,a_i,\ldots,a_j,\ldots,a_s)
\mapsto
(a_1,\ldots,a_i-1,\ldots,a_j+1,\ldots,a_s).
\]

The level is preserved:

\[
\Omega(N_{i\to j})=\Omega(N)=r.
\]

At a relative-interior point of an \(s\)-axis face there are

\[
\boxed{s(s-1)}
\]

ordered primitive horizontal neighbors. Hence the interior degrees are

\[
2,6,12,20,30,\ldots
\]

for \(s=2,3,4,5,6,\ldots\).

---

## 5. Root-lattice structure

The primitive transfer directions are

\[
e_j-e_i.
\]

All horizontal directions lie in

\[
A_{s-1}=\left\{h\in\mathbb Z^s:\sum_i h_i=0\right\}.
\]

Therefore a fixed-level prime-face section is a finite simplex cut from the root lattice \(A_{s-1}\):

- two axes: \(A_1\), a line segment;
- three axes: \(A_2\), a triangular lattice with six interior directions;
- four axes: \(A_3\), a tetrahedral section;
- \(s\) axes: a finite \((s-1)\)-simplex in \(A_{s-1}\).

This is an exact geometric reinterpretation of exponent transfers.

---

## 6. Horizontal directions are geometric sequences

Let

\[
h=(h_1,\ldots,h_s)\in A_{s-1}
\]

and define

\[
Q(h)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{h_i}.
\]

Whenever all exponent coordinates remain nonnegative,

\[
N_k=NQ(h)^k
\]

is an integer point in the same horizontal level. Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{horizontal lattice lines are finite geometric sequences}.}
\]

For the primitive direction \(e_j-e_i\), the ratio is

\[
Q=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

The sequence is finite inside the simplex because a donor exponent eventually reaches zero.

---

## 7. Complete pair-line through a point

Fix axes \(p_i,p_j\). The full horizontal line through \(N\), oriented from \(p_i\) to \(p_j\), is

\[
N_k=N\left(\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)^k,
\qquad -a_j\le k\le a_i.
\]

The endpoints occur when one of those two exponents becomes zero. Exponents on all other axes remain fixed.

For a two-axis face this line is the whole level. In higher faces it is one edge-parallel slice through the simplex.

---

## 8. Three different geometric-sequence families

### 8.1 Axis-parallel sequence

\[
N,\ Np_i,\ Np_i^2,\ldots
\]

has ratio \(p_i\) and rises through successive \(\Omega\)-levels.

### 8.2 Horizontal ratio sequence

\[
N\left(\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)^k
\]

has ratio \(p_j/p_i\) and remains in one level while all coordinates remain nonnegative.

### 8.3 Primitive composite ray

If

\[
N=u^d
\]

with primitive exponent vector for \(u\), then

\[
1,u,u^2,u^3,\ldots
\]

is the unique primitive ray containing \(N\).

These families must not be conflated: one adds height on one axis, one redistributes fixed height, and one scales an entire primitive direction.

---

## 9. Recovering local axes from an adjacent pair

Suppose \(M\) is a primitive horizontal neighbor of \(N\). Then

\[
N=gp_i,\qquad M=gp_j
\]

for a common base \(g\). Therefore

\[
g=\gcd(N,M)
\]

and

\[
\boxed{p_i=\frac{N}{g},\qquad p_j=\frac{M}{g}.}
\]

Thus a certified adjacent pair reveals its two local prime axes using one gcd and two divisions.

Conversely, if the reduced quotients

\[
\frac{N}{\gcd(N,M)},\qquad \frac{M}{\gcd(N,M)}
\]

are distinct primes, then the pair differs by one primitive valuation transfer and lies in the same \(\Omega\)-level.

This is an exact local inverse rule. It does not imply that a useful neighbor can always be generated from an unfactored isolated integer.

---

## 10. Non-adjacent points and horizontal distance

If two same-level points have exponent vectors \(a,b\), then

\[
h=b-a,\qquad \sum_i h_i=0
\]

and

\[
\frac{M}{N}=\prod_i p_i^{h_i}.
\]

The minimum number of primitive unit transfers is

\[
\boxed{d_H(N,M)=\frac12\sum_i|a_i-b_i|.}
\]

For a two-axis face, points separated by \(t\) steps have

\[
N=gp^t,\qquad M=gq^t,
\]

so

\[
\frac{M}{N}=\left(\frac qp\right)^t.
\]

---

## 11. Vertices of the horizontal simplex

For \(N=\prod p_i^{a_i}\) at level \(r\), the vertices are

\[
p_1^r,\ldots,p_s^r.
\]

The vertex on axis \(p_i\) is obtained by transferring all other exponents to it:

\[
\boxed{
p_i^r=N\prod_{j\ne i}\left(\frac{p_i}{p_j}\right)^{a_j}.
}
\]

Thus the surrounding axis vertices are determined once the support and exponents are known.

---

## 12. Valuation midpoints and geometric means

If

\[
\nu(C)=\frac{\nu(A)+\nu(B)}2
\]

is integral coordinatewise, then

\[
C^2=AB.
\]

So a valuation midpoint is a geometric mean:

\[
C=\sqrt{AB}.
\]

More generally, an integral barycentric relation

\[
m\nu(C)=m_1\nu(A_1)+\cdots+m_t\nu(A_t),
\qquad \sum_jm_j=m,
\]

becomes

\[
C^m=A_1^{m_1}\cdots A_t^{m_t}.
\]

---

## 13. Arithmetic signature of a primitive edge

For adjacent points

\[
N=gp_i,\qquad M=gp_j,
\]

we have

\[
\gcd(N,M)=g,
\]

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(N,M)=gp_ip_j,
\]

\[
N+M=g(p_i+p_j),
\]

\[
M-N=g(p_j-p_i).
\]

After division by the common base, the primitive edge signature is

\[
\boxed{1:p_i:p_j:(p_i+p_j):|p_j-p_i|:(p_ip_j).}
\]

The geometry of the edge is universal, while the arithmetic transition depends on \((p_i,p_j)\). Prime factors of \(p_i+p_j\) determine the axes entered by addition; prime factors of \(|p_j-p_i|\) determine the axes entered by subtraction.

Direction preservation, level preservation, and support preservation are different statements.

## 14. Exact examples

### 14.1 Point \(6\) in the \(2,3\) face

\[
6=2\cdot3,\qquad \Omega=2.
\]

Its horizontal line is

\[
4,6,9
\]

with ratio \(3/2\). Its primitive horizontal neighbors are

\[
6\frac23=4,\qquad 6\frac32=9.
\]

Axis-parallel sequences are \(6\cdot2^k\) and \(6\cdot3^k\); the primitive composite ray is \(6^k\).

### 14.2 Point \(10\) in the \(2,5\) face

\[
4,10,25
\]

is a horizontal geometric sequence of ratio \(5/2\).

### 14.3 Point \(21\) in the \(3,7\) face

\[
9,21,49
\]

is a horizontal geometric sequence of ratio \(7/3\).

### 14.4 Point \(30\) in the \(2,3,5\) face

\[
30=2\cdot3\cdot5,\qquad \Omega=3.
\]

Its six primitive horizontal neighbors are

\[
12,18,20,45,50,75.
\]

Its three vertices are

\[
8,27,125.
\]

The local graph is the six-direction neighborhood of \(A_2\), truncated by the triangular level simplex.

## 15. Local inverse-geometry question card

Given an integer point \(N\), ask:

1. Is the exact factorization known?
2. What is the minimal labeled support face?
3. What is \(\Omega(N)\)?
4. What is the exponent partition and primitive ray?
5. What are the vertices \(p_i^{\Omega(N)}\)?
6. What are the ordered axis ratios \(p_j/p_i\)?
7. What are the primitive horizontal neighbors \(Np_j/p_i\)?
8. What complete pair-lines pass through the point?
9. What axis-parallel sequences \(Np_i^k\) pass through it?
10. What composite ray \(u^k\) contains it?
11. If a candidate neighbor \(M\) is observed, what does \(\gcd(N,M)\) recover?
12. Does addition preserve the level, or does \(p_i+p_j\) add valuation mass?
13. Does subtraction introduce new axes through \(|p_j-p_i|\)?
14. Which arithmetic functions remain simple along each direction?
15. Which claims are identities, diagnostics, or open research questions?

- classify the finite horizontal neighborhood of a factored integer;
- generate every primitive transfer neighbor inside its support face;
- recover two local axes from a certified adjacent pair;
- compute horizontal graph distance from valuation vectors;
- generate axis-parallel, horizontal-ratio, and primitive-ray sequences;
- identify support transitions caused by local sums and differences;
- separate size, shape, support, level, and direction.

- search for recurring local signatures of arithmetic functions;
- compare two-axis and higher-dimensional faces;
- test whether structured neighbor/gcd probes expose factors in special families;
- study random walks and graph spectra on finite level simplices;
- classify factors of \(Q^t\pm1\) by the direction that generated them.

## 19. Governed research tracks

### Track A — finite-face geometry

Enumerate points, edges, distances, boundaries, direction classes, adjacency matrices, and graph spectra in small \(\Sigma_r(P)\).

**Classification:** identities / finite combinatorics / reinterpretation.

### Track B — arithmetic functions on directions

Study changes of

\[
\tau,\sigma,\varphi,\mu,\lambda,\omega,\Omega
\]

under

\[
N\mapsto N\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

**Classification:** identities where derived; otherwise finite diagnostics.

### Track C — additive and subtractive transitions

Classify factors of

\[
p_i+p_j,\quad |p_j-p_i|,\quad Q^t+1,\quad Q^t-1
\]

and build a transition graph between support faces.

**Classification:** exact reduction plus open distribution questions.

### Track D — local inverse problems

Study what can be reconstructed from neighbors, ratios, gcd data, short progressions, unlabeled distances, and partial factorizations. Every experiment must record information loss and counterexamples.

**Classification:** inverse-problem program.

### Track E — factorization diagnostics

Generate structured candidate points \(M\), test \(\gcd(N,M)\), and benchmark against trial division, Fermat, Pollard rho, ECM, and other appropriate baselines.

**Current status:** exploratory diagnostic only; no mechanism or speedup claim is authorized without a preregistered benchmark certificate.

### Track F — analytic distribution

Count integers by face dimension, level, exponent shape, primitive ray, boundary depth, and local-neighborhood signature, using explicit PVG-to-ANT translations and analytic certificates.

**Classification:** research front; the geometry supplies no analytic estimates by itself.

The detailed research agenda is maintained in `research/pvg-space-deepening/README.md`.

## 20. Central conclusion

The simple drawing exposes a coherent structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prime axes}
+\text{simplex levels}
+\text{root-lattice directions}
+\text{axis ratios}
+\text{geometric sequences}
+\text{local arithmetic transitions}.
}
\]

The project must preserve the distinction between an exact identity, a geometric reinterpretation, a finite pattern, an exploratory diagnostic, and an open theorem-level question. The space justifies systematic study, but every escalation requires proof, benchmark, and source checks rather than visual surprise alone.
