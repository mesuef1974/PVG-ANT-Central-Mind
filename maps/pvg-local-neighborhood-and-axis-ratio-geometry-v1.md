## 1. Purpose

The exact passport answers where an integer point lives. The local-neighborhood layer asks a second family of questions:

1. Which points are its nearest horizontal neighbors?
2. Which axis ratios generate those neighbors?
3. Which geometric sequences pass through the point?
4. Can an observed neighbor reveal active prime axes?
5. How do addition, subtraction, gcd, and lcm transform a local edge?
6. What survives when the face has three, four, or more prime axes?

The point is not treated as isolated. It is treated as a node in a finite horizontal simplex, an infinite family of axis-parallel rays, one primitive composite ray, and several arithmetic transition graphs.

---

## 2. Exact setup

Let

\[
N=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{a_i},\qquad a_i>0,
\]

with labeled support

\[
P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_s\}
\]

and level

\[
r=\Omega(N)=a_1+\cdots+a_s.
\]

The horizontal section of the face generated by \(P\) is

\[
\Sigma_r(P)=\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{x_i}:x_i\in\mathbb N_0,\ \sum_i x_i=r\right\}.
\]

Its exponent vectors form the lattice points of an \((s-1)\)-simplex.

---

## 3. Axis-ratio matrix

For every ordered pair of distinct active axes define

\[
R_{ij}=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

These ratios satisfy the exact cocycle laws

\[
R_{ij}R_{jk}=R_{ik},\qquad R_{ij}R_{ji}=1,
\]

and every closed axis cycle has product \(1\).

Thus axis ratios are not unrelated numerical fractions. They are the multiplicative image of coordinate-transfer directions.

---

## 4. Primitive horizontal neighbors

If \(a_i>0\), transfer one valuation unit from axis \(p_i\) to axis \(p_j\):

\[
N_{i\to j}=N\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

In exponent coordinates this is

\[
(a_1,\ldots,a_i,\ldots,a_j,\ldots,a_s)
\mapsto
(a_1,\ldots,a_i-1,\ldots,a_j+1,\ldots,a_s).
\]

The level is preserved:

\[
\Omega(N_{i\to j})=\Omega(N)=r.
\]

At a relative-interior point of an \(s\)-axis face there are

\[
\boxed{s(s-1)}
\]

ordered primitive horizontal neighbors.

Hence the interior degrees are

\[
2,6,12,20,30,\ldots
\]

for \(s=2,3,4,5,6,\ldots\).

On a boundary of a chosen ambient face, donor coordinates equal to zero remove the corresponding outgoing directions.

---

## 5. The root-lattice structure

The primitive transfer directions are

\[
e_j-e_i.
\]

All horizontal directions lie in

\[
A_{s-1}=\left\{h\in\mathbb Z^s:\sum_i h_i=0\right\}.
\]

Therefore a fixed-level prime-face section is a finite simplex cut from the root lattice \(A_{s-1}\).

This gives the dimension sequence:

- two active axes: \(A_1\), a line segment;
- three active axes: \(A_2\), a triangular/hexagonal local lattice;
- four active axes: \(A_3\), a tetrahedral section;
- \(s\) active axes: a finite \((s-1)\)-simplex in \(A_{s-1}\).

This is an exact geometric reinterpretation of exponent transfers.

---

## 6. Every horizontal direction is a geometric sequence

Let

\[
h=(h_1,\ldots,h_s)\in A_{s-1}.
\]

Define

\[
Q(h)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{h_i}.
\]

Whenever all exponent coordinates remain nonnegative,

\[
N_k=NQ(h)^k
\]

is an integer point in the same horizontal level.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{horizontal lattice lines are finite geometric sequences}.}
\]

For the primitive direction \(e_j-e_i\), the ratio is exactly

\[
Q=\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

The sequence is finite inside the simplex because one donor exponent eventually reaches zero.

---

## 7. Complete pair-line through a point

Fix axes \(p_i,p_j\). The full horizontal line through \(N\) in direction \(p_i\to p_j\) is indexed by

\[
-a_j\le k\le a_i
\]

and has terms

\[
N_k=N\left(\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)^k.
\]

The endpoints occur when one of the two exponents becomes zero. The remaining exponents on all other axes stay fixed.

For two-axis faces this line is the whole level. For higher faces it is one edge-parallel slice through the simplex.

---

## 8. Axis-parallel and composite-ray sequences

Three kinds of geometric sequence must not be conflated.

### 8.1 Axis-parallel sequence

\[
N,\ Np_i,\ Np_i^2,\ldots
\]

has ratio \(p_i\) and rises through successive \(\Omega\)-levels.

### 8.2 Horizontal ratio sequence

\[
N\left(\frac{p_j}{p_i}\right)^k
\]

has ratio \(p_j/p_i\) and remains in one \(\Omega\)-level while integer coordinates remain nonnegative.

### 8.3 Primitive composite ray

If

\[
N=u^d
\]

with primitive exponent vector for \(u\), then

\[
1,u,u^2,u^3,\ldots
\]

is the unique primitive ray containing \(N\).

The first sequence preserves all old coordinates and adds one axis height. The second redistributes fixed total height. The third scales the entire primitive direction.

---

## 9. Recovering local axes from an adjacent pair

Suppose \(M\) is a primitive horizontal neighbor of \(N\). Then for some common base \(g\),

\[
N=gp_i,\qquad M=gp_j.
\]

Therefore

\[
g=\gcd(N,M),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{p_i=\frac{N}{g},\qquad p_j=\frac{M}{g}.}
\]

Hence an observed adjacent pair reveals its two local prime axes by one gcd and two divisions, provided the reduced quotients are prime.

Conversely, if

\[
\frac{N}{\gcd(N,M)},\qquad \frac{M}{\gcd(N,M)}
\]

are distinct primes, then \(N,M\) differ by one primitive valuation transfer and lie in the same \(\Omega\)-level.

This is an exact local inverse rule. It does not imply that a useful neighbor can always be generated from an unfactored isolated integer.

---

## 10. Non-adjacent points and lattice distance

If two points in the same labeled face and level have exponent vectors \(a,b\), then

\[
h=b-a,\qquad \sum_i h_i=0,
\]

and

\[
\frac{M}{N}=\prod_i p_i^{h_i}.
\]

The minimum number of primitive unit transfers needed to move from \(N\) to \(M\) is

\[
\boxed{d_H(N,M)=\frac12\sum_i|a_i-b_i|.}
\]

This is the graph distance in the horizontal neighbor graph.

For a two-axis face, points separated by \(h\) steps have

\[
N=gp^h,\qquad M=gq^h,
\]

so

\[
\frac{M}{N}=\left(\frac qp\right)^h.
\]

---

## 11. Vertices of the point's horizontal simplex

For \(N=\prod p_i^{a_i}\) at level \(r\), the vertices of its labeled horizontal simplex are

\[
p_1^r,\ldots,p_s^r.
\]

The vertex on axis \(p_i\) is obtained by transferring all other exponents to \(p_i\):

\[
\boxed{
p_i^r=N\prod_{j\ne i}\left(\frac{p_i}{p_j}\right)^{a_j}.
}
\]

Thus once the support and exponents are known, the surrounding axis vertices are determined exactly.

---

## 12. Linear geometry becomes geometric means

The valuation map converts multiplication to vector addition. Consequently, when

\[
\nu(C)=\frac{\nu(A)+\nu(B)}{2}
\]

is integral coordinatewise,

\[
C^2=AB.
\]

So a valuation midpoint is an arithmetic geometric mean:

\[
C=\sqrt{AB}.
\]

More generally, an integral barycentric relation

\[
m\nu(C)=m_1\nu(A_1)+\cdots+m_t\nu(A_t),\qquad \sum_jm_j=m,
\]

becomes

\[
C^m=A_1^{m_1}\cdots A_t^{m_t}.
\]

This is a useful bridge between simplex geometry and multiplicative identities.

---

## 13. Arithmetic signature of a primitive edge

For adjacent points

\[
N=gp_i,\qquad M=gp_j,
\]

we have

\[
\gcd(N,M)=g,
\]

\[
\operatorname{lcm}(N,M)=gp_ip_j,
\]

\[
N+M=g(p_i+p_j),
\]

\[
M-N=g(p_j-p_i).
\]

After dividing by the common base, the complete primitive edge signature is

\[
\boxed{
1:p_i:p_j:(p_i+p_j):|p_j-p_i|:(p_ip_j).
}
\]

The geometry of the edge is universal; the arithmetic transition depends on the pair \((p_i,p_j)\).

The prime factors of \(p_i+p_j\) determine the support entered by addition. The prime factors of \(|p_j-p_i|\) determine the support entered by subtraction.

Therefore horizontal direction preservation, level preservation, and support preservation are three different questions.

## 14. Exact examples

### 14.1 Point \(6\) in the \(2,3\) face

\[
6=2\cdot3,\qquad \Omega=2.
\]

Horizontal line:

\[
4,6,9
\]

with ratio \(3/2\). Primitive neighbors are

\[
6\frac23=4,\qquad 6\frac32=9.
\]

Axis-parallel sequences are

\[
6\cdot2^k,\qquad 6\cdot3^k,
\]

and the primitive composite ray is \(6^k\).

### 14.2 Point \(10\) in the \(2,5\) face

\[
4,10,25
\]

is a horizontal geometric sequence of ratio \(5/2\).

### 14.3 Point \(21\) in the \(3,7\) face

\[
9,21,49
\]

is a horizontal geometric sequence of ratio \(7/3\).

### 14.4 Point \(30\) in the \(2,3,5\) face

\[
30=2\cdot3\cdot5,\qquad \Omega=3.
\]

Its six primitive horizontal neighbors are

\[
12,18,20,45,50,75.
\]

The three vertices are

\[
8,27,125.
\]

The local graph is the six-direction neighborhood of the \(A_2\) lattice, truncated by the triangular level simplex.

## 15. Local inverse-geometry question card

Given an integer point \(N\), ask:

1. Is the exact factorization known?
2. What is the minimal labeled support face?
3. What is \(\Omega(N)\)?
4. What is the exponent partition and primitive ray?
5. What are the axis vertices \(p_i^{\Omega(N)}\)?
6. What are the ordered axis ratios \(p_j/p_i\)?
7. What are the primitive horizontal neighbors \(N p_j/p_i\)?
8. What complete pair-lines pass through the point?
9. What axis-parallel sequences \(Np_i^k\) pass through it?
10. What composite ray \(u^k\) contains it?
11. If a candidate neighbor \(M\) is observed, what does \(\gcd(N,M)\) recover?
12. Does addition preserve the level, or does \(p_i+p_j\) introduce extra valuation mass?
13. Does subtraction introduce new axes through \(|p_j-p_i|\)?
14. Which arithmetic functions remain simple along each direction?
15. Which claims are exact identities, finite diagnostics, or open research questions?

## 16. What this can be used for now

### Exact capabilities

- classify the finite horizontal neighborhood of a factored integer;
- generate every primitive transfer neighbor inside a chosen support face;
- recover the two local axes from a certified adjacent pair;
- compute horizontal graph distances from valuation vectors;
- generate axis-parallel, horizontal-ratio, and primitive-ray geometric sequences;
- identify the support transitions caused by local sums and differences;
- separate size, shape, support, level, and direction.

### Diagnostic capabilities

- search for recurring local signatures of arithmetic functions;
- compare faces \((p,q)\) and higher-dimensional prime supports;
- test whether neighbor-generated gcd probes expose factors in special families;
- study random walks and spectral statistics on finite level simplices;
- classify factors of \(Q^h\pm1\) by the direction \(Q\) that generated them.

### Not established

- no general factorization speedup;
- no primality proof from failure to find neighbors;
- no analytic estimate from visualization alone;
- no novelty or publication claim;
- no Goldbach, RH, or GRH progress.

## 17. Governed research agenda

### Track A — exact finite-face geometry

1. Enumerate all points, edges, distances, boundaries, and direction classes in \(\Sigma_r(P)\).
2. Derive adjacency matrices and graph spectra for small \(s,r\).
3. Separate labeled-prime geometry from unlabeled exponent-shape quotients.
4. Study midpoint and barycentric multiplicative identities.

**Classification:** identities / finite combinatorics / reinterpretation.

### Track B — arithmetic functions on directions

For functions such as

\[
\tau,\ \sigma,\ \varphi,\ \mu,\ \lambda,\ \omega,\ \Omega,
\]

measure changes under

\[
N\mapsto N\frac{p_j}{p_i}.
\]

Determine which observables are linear, convex, monotone, periodic, or irregular along horizontal lines.

**Classification:** known identities where derivable; otherwise finite diagnostic until proved.

### Track C — additive and subtractive support transitions

Classify the factorizations of

\[
p_i+p_j,\qquad |p_j-p_i|,
\]

and more generally

\[
Q^h+1,\qquad Q^h-1.
\]

Build a transition graph between support faces.

**Classification:** exact reduction plus open distribution questions.

### Track D — local inverse problems

Study how much of a point's passport can be reconstructed from:

- one or more neighbors;
- ratios only;
- gcd data;
- a short geometric progression;
- unlabeled distances;
- partial factorization.

Every experiment must report information loss and counterexamples.

**Classification:** inverse-problem program.

### Track E — factorization diagnostics

Generate structured candidate points \(M\) and test

\[
\gcd(N,M).
\]

Benchmark honestly against trial division, Fermat, Pollard rho, ECM, and other appropriate baselines. A geometric description alone is not an algorithmic gain.

**Classification:** candidate mechanism / benchmark required.

### Track F — analytic distribution

Count integers up to \(x\) by face dimension, level, exponent shape, primitive ray, boundary depth, and local-neighborhood signatures. Connect only through explicit ANT translations and certificates.

**Classification:** research front; analytic estimates not supplied by the geometry.

## 18. Central conclusion

The simple drawing exposes a coherent structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prime axes}
+\text{simplex levels}
+\text{root-lattice directions}
+\text{axis ratios}
+\text{geometric sequences}
+\text{local arithmetic transitions}.
}
\]

The important methodological rule is to preserve the distinction between:

- an exact identity already implied by valuations;
- a useful geometric reinterpretation;
- a finite computational pattern;
- a candidate mechanism;
- an open theorem-level question.

The space is rich enough to justify systematic study, but every escalation must pass proof, benchmark, and source checks rather than relying on visual surprise alone.
